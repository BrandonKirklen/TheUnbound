# Setting

Conjured Temple of the Matron of Ravens, Whitestone

![The temple of the night rising from the moonlit grounds outside Whitestone](Whitestone%20Funeral%20-%20Temple%20Exterior.jpg)

![The Sun Tree in Whitestone, the temple of the dawn nestled at its roots](Sun%20Tree%20in%20Whitestone.jpeg)

Setting: On the outskirts of the city nestled into the Parchwood timbers, with the illustrious Sun Tree peaking over the treeline in the distance, near the temple of the dawn, Whitestone’s cobbles are rimmed with chalk sigils and raven feathers. At Tomis’s call, a temple of the night unfolds from hush and moonlight, a transient sanctum layered over the square contrasting with the bright, warm and inviting temple of the dawn nearby. Pillars of smoked glass rise, rooted to the ground with trunk-like pillars to the ground, veined with silver like frost, and a vaulted nave joins the night. Shadows gather thick at the eaves, moving as if they breathe. It seems more like a stony extension of the forest, Tomis’s homeland connected and covered by the same canopy that’s extended nearby.

![The obsidian altar suspended above the blood-dark pool, ringed by amethyst lanterns](Whitestone%20Funeral%20-%20Empty%20Altar.jpg)

The temple is shaped in an amphitheater, sloping down to a deep center pit in the middle of a forest of stone. In the center stands a great obsidian altar, suspended on arched bridges above a wide pool that reflects dark blood. Amethyst lanterns cast violet halos that skim the surface. The castle bells are silent. It feels as though we’re all waiting for someone to exhale and set the shadows pulsing.

# Ceremony

![The mourners gathered around the altar as the ceremony begins](Whitestone%20Funeral%20-%20Ceremony.jpg)

## Opening Invocation

With the brilliance of the setting sun lighting up the dawnfather square, Tomis approaches from the wings, kneels before the pool, and says in a quiet voice that carries through the square before the temple

“Matron of Ravens,  
Keeper of our final flight,  
You who see every thread in the tapestry of fate,  
Bear witness to these stories completed

Help us let them set their burdens set down  
Their struggles new finished  
Guide them to their final homes  
And stand with us as we learn to let them go

He rises and approaches the altar.

## Boon and a Burden

“Thank you all for joining me here, I’ve recently been able to do a bit of magic, and I couldn’t think of a better reason to spend it than for why we’re here today and to commemorate the transition of so many souls that have meant so much to me. To us of the unbound. To all of us that call these woods home. And to all our friends from across the world who came to help.

*Tomis takes a deep breath, steadying himself as the brilliance of the tree in dawnfather square wanes and begins to fade in the evening twilight. The shadows of the temple seem to breathe in too, swelling and growing larger and darker.* 

A hundred and thirty three days ago, I was given a boon, and a burden.  
The boon, to step outside the flow of fate, to walk beyond the Weave and see the threads that bind us.  
The burden, to keep them alive, to gather strength, to grow our bonds, and by that work prevent the deaths of many.

For months, we pulled at those threads, sometimes gently, sometimes with reckless desperation.  
With every tug came consequence.  
Ravagers struck those closest to me.  
Great leaders and simple folk alike bled.  
Friends left. Cities rose from the ground and fled the sky.  
An inevitable usurpation, and a likely cruel and horrific afterlife loomed in the possible future  
And for every life I saved trying to keep this future at bay, it felt like another life had slipped away.

And that feeling reached its peak this last week.  
This last week I believed I had failed that charge.  
I could not keep them alive.   
My pull on fate faltered, and I faced an impossible choice.  
And in that moment, I chose to stop pulling.  
To stop trying to drag the world my way.  
And instead let the threads that bind us all be tested.

Even in that surrender, those threads held strong, and the world changed.”

*Amethyst light pares back the twilight darkness, setting the still anticipatory shadows dancing from behind the darkened altar.* 

What was once inevitable became only likely.  
What was once likely became only possible.

The cost was heavy, yet the deliverance of many was born from the sacrifice of a few.

I want to tell you about those few strong threads today and in so doing, hopefully weave them into our tapestry of life even more tightly.

After a week, tedious.

After our first journey together, comforting.  
At the last rescue, life saving.

Those rules became our creed.”

I’ve asked my friend Uriel to read these rules today for us.

## Rayleen’s Rules (Uriel read this)

* You are never more than thirty seconds from battle readiness.  
* Do not piss each other off. If I have to deal with it, I take half of both your pay for my trouble.  
* Listen to the senior crew. We will not ask twice.  
* Senior crew and engineers do not go with adventurers off the ship. I advise none of you do so.  
* We are a family. Act like one, our lives may depend on it.  
* We do not abandon our own, ever.

## The Hands That Built

“Among those who kept the *Deliverance* aloft were two souls whose hands shaped her spirit as much as her hull.

Pyrrhus Alonas, artificer, tinkerer, noble of Westruun, who saw in the impossible the architecture of salvation.  
He bent brass and breath alike to her service, carving light into weaponry and warmth into steel.  
To him, the ship was never just wood and rigging. It was a promise, that ingenuity could be mercy’s equal.

And Cassidy Bell, a farmer turned engineer, who traded plow and seed for powder and storm.  
She sowed her courage in every bolt she tightened, every coil she wound, every shot fired.  
Where Pyrrhus gave the *Deliverance* its mind, Cassidy gave it its heart, every time striking true.

Their work made her more than a vessel.  
She became a living testament to what we can build together”

## Whitestone (Rowan)

Speaking of building together, Rayleen and us wouldn’t have even met without Whitestone’s foresight. Fathers and mothers, sons and daughters, farmers, tinkerers, bright minds looking to a better future,   
took up the call to be part of something bigger, a cord that couldn’t be broken. Your people endured loss without turning away, even when the sky itself seemed to fall, held strong. 

I’ve asked my friend Rowan to say a few more words about this strong thread. 

## Ashari (Uriel)

A force of nature in this fight were the Ashari. I’ve had the pleasure to walk with one of their most valiant, Earth Warden of the Ashari over the last 133 days, I’ve watched him grow and and refused to let him leave. 

I’ve asked him to say a few more words about this strong thread. 

## Kraghammer (Grimbold)

With goats along the rim, rifles braced against stone and discipline to stand fast while the world shook beneath their feet, we could not have prevailed without the Dwarves, Gnomes, and Humans of Kraghammer. 

I’ve asked my friend Grimbold to say a bit about their stony strong thread. 

## Marquet (Freya)

And from beyond our borders, from skies not sworn to Tal’Dorei, the Sky Defense League and Alsferan Union answered fearsome and brave. A debt is now written in wind and wake, and it will not be forgotten. I’ve asked my fearsomely brave friend Freya to say a bit about their lofty strong thread. 

## Everyone Else (Tomis)

These rules were not chains. They were threads that bound us together.  
This ship was not just a vessel. This fight was not just a battle.  
It was a great entanglement of strong souls.  
Through them and the lives willingly sacrificed, the Matron focused her strength upon her champion.  
Through them, her will endured while the world tried to bleed her dry.

The *Deliverance* and her crew held the line, not for glory, not for vengeance,  
but so others could live and die with dignity.

And live we shall.”

To the crew of the *Deliverance,* the people of Whitestone, the ashari, the people of kraghammer, the people of Marquet, and every other force that joined with us — you have delivered us all still.  
While your sacrifice ended your stories, for us it let us begin the next verse.  
With a fate now bent to your will.

## Closing Benediction

“Matron of Ravens,  
Receive them into your warm embrace.  
Their threads are tied, yet their pattern remains,  
woven into all who live because they lived.”

“Keep them alive, my child. That was the charge.  
So we will keep them alive, by memory, by story, by faith.”
