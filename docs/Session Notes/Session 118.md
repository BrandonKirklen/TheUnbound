### Previously On: The Unbound

The party ascended into the Alabaster Sierras under cover of Tomis's Control Weather, scaling one of the southern peaks to the ruins of an ancient observatory. There they found a **Nagpa** flanked by a relatively small Aarakocra guard — and the fight that followed was the hardest the party has faced.

Almost immediately, every member was caught in a spell of a magnitude the modern age has not known. **Freya was disintegrated** outright. Rowan was hammered to the brink of death and back. The Nagpa's illusions and corrupting magic turned the battlefield against them — at one point **Grimbold** fell fully under its thrall and blasted **Tomis** with one of his most powerful spells. Once the party broke through the illusion and forced the spell to drop, Rowan landed the killing blow on the Nagpa.

But the Aarakocra had been ignored in the rush to kill the caster, and the captain struck down two of the party in the aftermath — **Uriel**, who had spent everything trying to bring the Nagpa down, and Rowan. Tomis revivified Rowan on the spot. When the ash settled, three of the party were dead, one of them disintegrated beyond ordinary recall.

The session opens in the ruins, in the rising winds of a building monsoon, with Freya gone to dust, Uriel dead in the bag of holding, and no one in good shape.

### Session Notes

#### Looting the Observatory

- Grimbold wept over Uriel's body, then stood, wiped the ashen mud that had been Freya from his hands onto his robes, and set to investigating the region quickly before the weather cleared.
- Tomis kept concentration on Control Weather, holding the storm and the gale-force winds in place over the peak. He picked up the scroll left where Freya fell — the **fortune that had once read "Your fiery passion burns too hot"** — and found it had transformed into a **scroll of Revivify**.
  - Tomis to Rowan: "That fortune, it turned into a scroll here for revivification. I can't use it on Freya, but I could try it on Uriel."
- Tomis attempted to gather Freya's ashes, but the swirling winds scattered them almost immediately into the stream of feathers circling the peaks. Only her heavier equipment remained terrestrial.
- Tomis stowed **Uriel's body**, the **Revivify scroll**, the **Aarakocra captain's plate mail** (took ~10 minutes to don off the corpse, ultimately just stuffed the whole body in), and **Freya's gear (axe, amulet, a book)** in the bag of holding.
  - **Bag of holding correction:** confirmed size 4 — content weight limit **1,500 lbs**, **250 cubic feet** of volume, bag itself weighs ~60 lbs. Both corpses fit but neared the cap.
- The Aarakocra captain wore well-fitted plate, soaked in blood; his massive **greataxe** lay caked in blood nearby. (Uriel later noted the captain's sword "hurt a lot.")

#### The Nagpa's Body

- Rowan searched the Nagpa and found it disturbingly bare. No spellbook, no foci, no components, no personal effects of note — nothing a powerful arcanist would normally carry.
- With the illusory veil dropped, the Nagpa was revealed as a pathetic, wretched creature in tattered beggar's rags. Its small bundle held filthy scraps of food, including a half-eaten rat it had gnawed and saved.
- **Overtly magical:** a **crown-looking object** adorning its head.
- **Not immediately identifiable:** **three identical, well-crafted metal rods**, roughly two feet long, each appearing splintered or broken — but cleanly, not jaggedly, and they do not thread or fit together. They look incomplete, yet identical.

#### The Observatory and the Portal

- The observatory was identified as a long-derelict relic, likely dating to the **Age of Arcanum**. Vaguely Dwarven architecture, shattered telescope lenses, rotted-out roofing near collapse, and generations of Aarakocra nesting and droppings — territory held for years, not months.
- Rowan investigated the **portal to the Plane of Air** that Uriel had discovered. A clear blue sky was visible through it against the gray storm.
  - **Arcana 20:** the portal's shape is an odd middle ground — neither the geometric precision of a deliberately crafted ritual portal nor the natural tear of cosmic shifting. Rowan couldn't determine what would cause it.
  - Closing it is beyond any one caster. The Ashari maintain teams of druids dedicated to such work; it requires a full ritual with multiple powerful spellcasters. Grimbold and Tomis were both nearly tapped out, so the portal was left open.
- Tomis collected a chipped piece of stone iconography from the observatory in case the party wants to teleport back to investigate later.
- Tomis foraged a new **augury stick** from the Nagpa's nest material — something wet and "leggy," chosen for the wind that had inspired him.
- Rowan cast **Divine Sense** and **Truesight**; nothing beyond the expected pinged. The place was simply wretched.

#### Word of Recall to Vasselheim

- With Grimbold out of flight magic and no easy path down the mountain, the party chose to evacuate. Tomis dropped concentration on Control Weather, then cast **Word of Recall**, returning the three living members to the **Raven's Crest Temple** in Vasselheim — emerging, as always, through the **blood pool**.
- Rowan messaged Lieve'tel by ring before recall, faster than he'd ever heard a reply: "Nagpa killed. Portal to airplane still open. In Vasselheim. Uriel, Freya dead. Tomis, Rowan, Grimbold alive."
  - Lieve'tel's reply: she would inform those present of the success. She had been called to an **emergency meeting of the Dawn Marshals** and was away.

### Interlude: Freya and Asmodeus

While the rest of the party was in Vasselheim, the scene cut to Freya's disintegrated consciousness — and the figure who came to bargain with her.

- The figure presented as a well-groomed man of about 50, black chinstrap goatee, fine noble's robes, seated at a desk in a dusty study, writing.
- He revealed himself, obliquely, as the entity who had spoken to Freya through her axe — **Sky Sunder / "Luis"** — and ultimately as **Asmodeus** himself. ("The big one.")
- Freya wished to return to the material plane to take vengeance on those who took her wife and to bring her wife back to life.
- **The deal struck:**
  - Asmodeus would grant Freya the ability to be brought back, plus advisement on a road by which her wife might be restored — but he could not restore the wife himself, as "she does not dwell in my domain."
  - In exchange, Freya would serve **eternally as a soldier in the Blood War**, fighting demons. (Freya led with eternity herself; per the GM's note, Asmodeus had been prepared to negotiate for a fixed term.)
  - **Addendum Freya negotiated:** one full **50-year mortal lifespan** with her wife before being called to service — pegged at 50 years specifically so Asmodeus would have no incentive to extend the wife's life unnaturally or arrange an accident.
  - **Asmodeus's price clause:** should the party come to banish this "previous death god," Freya must **kill the avatar and land the final blow.** The 50 years is contingent on her striking that killing blow. "Banish him. Kill him, and be sure to strike the final blow."
- The contract was signed in blood and vanished in flame. Asmodeus restored Freya from her infirm state as a parting courtesy.
- **Critically:** the deal did **not** include resurrection to the material plane. Freya still needs the party to bring her body back by other means — something with the god of death's presence has been interfering with resurrection (as happened with Grog). The party is not meant to know the contract's terms.

### Back at the Raven's Crest

#### Kayvon and the Blood Pool

- The party emerged from the blood pool to find **Kayvon**, keeper of the inner chamber, giving them the usual disapproving look.
  - Grimbold: "Tomis, after this, can we please change your Word of Recall to a different location?"
  - Tomis: "Where else would we go? ... I can't do it anywhere else."
- Kayvon noted the other Dawn Marshals maintain **way stations down the thousand steps** and treat the climb up as a religious pilgrimage to cleanse themselves of the spell's residue before re-entering the Holy City. The Matron of Ravens' clergy has no such way station.
- Kayvon cast Create Water to wash the worst of the blood off the party before they ventured into the city — a deliberate callback for Tomis to the early days when he first bathed a dirt-caked Uriel the same way.
- Tomis reported the Nagpa kill. Kayvon: "Then there's still work left to be done" — two Nagpa remain.

#### The Night Visit to the Cobalt Archive

- Grimbold insisted on going to the Cobalt Archive that night to translate the Nagpa's strewn papers. The party agreed not to split up after the Myriad ambush; Tomis and Rowan accompanied him but were left waiting outside (the Archive is a vault first, a research facility second).
- A monk let Grimbold in to find his simulacrum, **Grimcold**, who had been researching in the archives for days — the study now a chaos of half-eaten meals and books, Grimcold face-planted asleep.
- **The flying cities, first mention:** the escorting monk let slip that "something was spotted this afternoon." Grimbold pressed and was redirected to higher authority.

#### Grimbold's Briefing from Grimcold — Banishing a God

Grimcold reported on what he'd been tasked to research: how Vox Machina banished Vecna, and what it would take to deal with the newly-arisen god of death.

- **The method:** Vox Machina forged **three prime trammels** and used them with the **Tome of Isolation**, which contains the **Rite of Banishment**.
  - The Tome of Isolation is held in the Vasselheim archives — inaccessible without the Head Archivist's and likely the Dawn Marshals' approval.
  - The ritual does not destroy or erase a god (as the Raven Queen attempted with the original death god); it **pushes them across the Divine Gate** to the other side, from which they cannot return. This is what was done to Vecna — he is still a god, just on the far side now.
  - There are mixed accounts of how badly reading the Tome tears apart the one who channels it. Grimcold suggested Grimbold may be best suited "with the wargs," and joked, "at least then my name will be in the history books."
- **The trammels amplify the Tome:** the more of the god (or its avatar) caught in their effect, the more likely the banishment succeeds. The danger: "you shoot at the king and you miss" — survival is very low if the banishment fails against a god on this side of the Gate.
  - **The detail not publicized about Vox Machina:** they only managed to get **two trammels** in; the third was made poorly. There was a very real chance Vecna wasn't banished and they died instead — they got lucky.
- **The Aeorian artifact angle:** rumors of an artifact built to *kill* gods — the **Extinguishment Chamber** in the underwater city (Aeor) the party visited. Its aspirational purpose was god-killing, but it was never tested to that point and was destroyed with the city. Worryingly, the city's destruction was **not** part of the enemy's plan, meaning they may have believed they had access to something like it.
- **Trammel whereabouts:** none known to exist currently. The one **broken** trammel was kept in the **Whitestone vault** and was the target of the heist by **Ioset and his party**, taken to the **League of Miracles** — which is why Grimcold suspects the League may have crafted more. No hard evidence either way, and no known method to make new ones.

#### The Raven Queen Concern (For Grimbold's Ears Only)

Grimcold raised a private worry he explicitly told Grimbold to keep from Tomis:

- For thousands of years the death domain has been an essentially 100–0 split: the Raven Queen held all of it, the Forgotten One none. If she could have simply killed him in her own domain of death, what better opportunity would there ever have been? She never did — suggesting it may not be possible.
- If the Forgotten One is banished across the Divine Gate, the Raven Queen — who has dwelt in the **Shadowfell** (this side of the Gate) the whole time — might make her presence more openly known on the material plane. With a visible, accessible god, she'd gather far more devotion than she ever has.
- Grimcold's deeper concern: this might do **more** than restore the status quo. The Raven Queen has historically kept a low profile and never demanded a large following — but a god walking openly and demanding, say, blood sacrifice, is exactly what the Divine Gate was built to prevent.
- Grimcold floated the possibility that banishing **both** gods might not be a terrible outcome — that it's what was supposed to happen at the end of the Age of Arcanum, but the Matron kept low enough that everyone forgot.
  - Grimbold, intrigued: "It would complete the cycle."

#### The Nagpa's Notes — A Dangerous Discovery

- Grimcold identified the script as **ancient Zarfelon** — a language from **Zarfell**, one of the powerful flying cradle-cities of the Age of Arcanum that no longer exists. (Arcana 30, on a nat 20; Tomis was excluded from these rolls — not for lack of relevant knowledge but for lack of the skill modifier to succeed.)
- **What the notes are:** not spells. **Magical theory more advanced than any current understanding of magic** — focused largely on the **Schools of Enchantment and Illusion**. Grimcold likened it to "a middle schooler being given a PhD dissertation." He believes no living spellcaster, except possibly the remaining Nagpa, would comprehend it.
  - "This wasn't a Nagpa's notes. ... You find virtually no two pieces of paper that are penned by the same hand." The Nagpa was a collector of this material, not its author.
- The crown is theorized to **transmit creativity from the brains of puppeted spellcasters** — the Nagpa, stripped of their own creativity by their curse, using captives as a source.
- Grimbold and Grimcold resolved to **keep the find secret**, including from the Cobalt Soul. Grimbold tucked the bulk of the ~50 pages into his locked spellbook and kept ~15 cover pages aside (those the curator had glimpsed).

#### The Curator's Interruption

- Grimbold had earlier sent for a high-ranking member to come speak with him; this brought **High Curator Veil Tesserim**, the **High Curator of Antiquity** — an older human, sharp-featured, woken in the middle of the night and visibly tired. (He is widely regarded as the likeliest successor to the Head Archivist.)
- **The flying cities, confirmed:** "Those flying cities you warned about have been spotted en route to Vasselheim." **Two cities**, spotted that afternoon, **2–3 days out** depending on velocity.
- Tesserim was dismissive of the Nagpa kill as a priority given the incoming threat ("Vasselheim has stood since before the calamity"), and would not get Grimbold into the Dawn Marshals' meeting, but agreed to pass word of the Nagpa's death to the Head Archivist via Sending.
- **The close call:** Tesserim rolled a **24 Perception** and clocked the enchanted vellum Grimbold was working on, recognizing it as historical material and offering to help translate. Grimbold deflected (Persuasion 20) and bought time until morning. Grimcold had hidden the key passages with a **Sleight of Hand 20**.
  - The Archive's texts are enchanted with alarm magic against defacement; ripping out pages would require first dispelling that enchantment. The party decided not to risk it and to stall the curator instead.
- As they left near dawn, Grimbold gained a **level of exhaustion** (failed Con save at disadvantage, rolled 12 vs DC 15).

### Resurrecting Uriel

Back at the Raven's Crest that evening, after a few meads, the party turned to Uriel. Tomis prepared the body on the altar before the blood pool and began the ritual, walking Rowan through the steadying rites.

> Matron of Ravens. Four times now. I know what I'm asking. The cracks from Uriel are continuing to worsen, but we need him. His work is not done. The Ashari have not finished what they've sent him to do, and he hasn't finished what he wants to do either.
>
> Uriel, Earthwarden of the Ashari, your watch has not ended yet. We still need you. Come back. Through the stone, through the breath — come back angry, come back cracked, but come back to us.
> — Tomis

- **Mechanics:** Revivify from the scroll, cast in the high temple of the Matron, by a cleric freshly granted a boon from her, using a scroll that is itself a creation of the Matron's church (the **Doomsday Cookie**, an ancient Matron recipe sold under the name of the **Dusk Maven**). The GM ruled this granted **advantage**.
  - This was **Uriel's 4th resurrection** (3rd death; the failed attempt during the Ioset party fight counts toward the resurrection-difficulty tally).
  - Tomis rolled the Wisdom check at advantage: **23. Success.**
- Uriel returned with a gasp, his already-cracked body breaking a little further as the cracks widened. Tomis rushed forward to embrace him.
  - Tomis: "Thank you."
  - Tomis followed with a Cure Wounds, putting Uriel at 10 HP.

### Campfire: Tomis, Rowan, and Willow

While Grimbold was at the Archive, Tomis and Rowan shared warm cider, mead, and porridge in the temple, and Rowan spoke at length about his late wife.

- **Willow** — the mage in the adventuring party that defeated **Sky Sunder** and the **Rime Lord** hundreds of years ago. She was the reason Rowan renounced his place in the **Feywild** (sent originally by **Lady Eleanor of the Seelie Court**) and became a citizen of the material plane.
- After their adventuring days, Willow and Rowan traveled the world helping people — her idea, her instinct. She coerced him into joining the **Arcana Pansophical**, which is part of why he still works there. They had three half-elf children — **Ash, Cedar, and Fern** — all long dead of age, with great-great-grandchildren now scattered across the world. Rowan visits each new addition to the family, liquidating adventuring wealth to support them through schooling.
- Rowan reflected that seasons pass for him as days do, and that he tends to lose track of time on his own. Tomis: "It does seem like you pay more attention to the eons than to the seasons."

Tomis spoke of his own history in turn:

- He met his mate **Lilic Tor** during an annual cull of the bull elk coming down from the snow. He overheard her whisper over a kill — **"Go gently, mother is waiting"** — before sending it on, and that was how she introduced him to the Matron.
- "She definitely taught me a lot, but I don't think I really appreciated her lessons until after Li was taken." After his son **El-li** was taken, Tomis couldn't be around his own kind and went north seeking revenge.
- On Lilic: still very fond of each other, still with the herd, but Tomis noted centaurs don't hold the same long-term monogamous bonds, and he isn't looking for any relationship after El-li.

The two discussed the morality of their enemies and their own work:

- Rowan observed that most villains believe they act for a greater good, and that the **League of Miracles** likely doesn't even understand the god of death they're serving — they're told they're bringing order to Tal'Dorei.
- Tomis held firm on his hatred regardless: "Maybe it's my own selfish, blind devotion to the Matron, but everything I've heard of this Forgotten One is worse." And later: "You may have opened my eyes as usual, Rowan, but I still can't set aside any of my vitriol for them."
- Both agreed to keep an eye on **Grimbold** — Tomis: "I don't want you having to go after him next."
