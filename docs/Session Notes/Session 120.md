### Recap

Brandon was absent last session, so Tomis sat out the party's arrival in Whitestone. To give him his moment, the DM rewound the clock to the private, early-morning conversation with Lieve'tel at the Raven's Crest in Vasselheim — the scene meant to open Session 119 — before the party teleported south. Lieve'tel had been summoned in the night to an emergency meeting of the Dawn Marshals, and called Tomis to her office at dawn to share what she learned: the Dawn Marshals intend to destroy the Titan, and the Matron of Ravens has been hiding inside it.

From that counsel the session widens back to Whitestone, where the reunited party presses the Council for their reward for slaying the Nagpa, raids the Treasury, buys out the magic shop — and learns, via Legend Lore, that three rods they took off the Nagpa are fragments of the trammels once used to banish Vecna. Tomis communes with the dead cleric Deliverance, the party abandons a Grey Valley plan after an Augury warns it is futile, and they teleport off to Grimbold's distant homeland of Niirdal-Poc, arriving to a guard's drawn polearm.

### Session Notes

### Rewind — The Dawn's Counsel (Vasselheim, the Raven's Crest)

- Tomis rose early, prepped, and went to Lieve'tel's office. The attendant **Kayvon**, finishing his overnight shift, gestured him inside.
- Lieve'tel — pale, full elf, near jet-black hair — was trancing when he entered, her eyes opening as she sensed his approach.

> Tomis: "Good morning, Lieve'tel. Pardon the interruption."

#### The Titan Must Fall

- **Word from the Dawn Marshals: they intend to destroy the Titan**, to prevent it being reactivated — fearing that leaving such a "hanging pendulum" (this nascent/previous god of death) over Vasselheim invites destruction with an attack possibly incoming.
- Why that is bad news: **the Matron has been hiding amid the Titan's remains.** Its powerful necromantic energy is one of the very few things large and potent enough to **mask her divine signature on this plane** — Lieve'tel's image: it takes a large enough object to block out even a bright flame.
- Tomis had mostly convinced himself she'd already moved on. "So do we need to find another space for her?" Lieve'tel: they need *some* solution — another place, or something else.
- Tomis: **"Is the Shadowfell safe for her?"** Lieve'tel: "It doesn't seem that way. She had to flee from her own home there."

#### To Hide, or to Rise

- Lieve'tel confessed she "grows tired of playing defense," and floated a harder path she hasn't yet put to the Matron:

> Lieve'tel: "Do you think it behooves the Matron to run and hide as this battle of belief rages across the world? If we are to convince the people that *she* is the god of death — not this other — is that message truly conveyed by her continuing to run and hide as whatever belief she has left is slowly sieved away?"

- The counter-risk, as Tomis named it, is exposing her presence. Lieve'tel argued that if the alternative is losing all remaining belief, exposure may be worth it — but it would require a **grand gesture** that announces her to the world and commands worship, mirroring what the enemy (the Forgotten One) is attempting. The party might not need to be told.

#### A Beacon Over the World

- Tomis suggested the gesture land more in **Tal'Dorei**, and raised commandeering a **flying city** as a beacon.
- Lieve'tel: the cities require a spellcaster / power source — she, Tomis, or **the Matron herself** could serve, and the Matron "could remake one of them in her image." A powerful beacon.
- Two candidates:
  - **Returning Emon (the Cloudtop District) to its footings** — attractive, but the larger task; Cloudtop reportedly still holds many of Emon's people.
  - **The Ravager Castle** — the smaller target (a castle and outlying areas). Taking it could also exploit the **fractures in the Ravager / League of Miracles alliance** and remove a clear and present danger.

#### The Grey Valley's Demonic Power

- Tomis noted the Ravager base came from the **Grey Valley**. Lieve'tel corrected a key point: its power is **not necromantic — "today we'd call them demonic," but truly suffused with ancient, deep magic.**
- She wouldn't know how to harness that much latent magic — but **the Matron ascended once via a Power Surge**, and could do something with such a source.
- Tomis recalled **Deliverance**, the Matron's devotee from **Jorenn Village**: "He died — but perhaps he knew more, or has learned more." Lieve'tel: if not, Tomis could survey the land himself.
- She framed it as one idea, promising to consult the Matron; meanwhile the **more palatable option to the party — simply relocating the Matron — is worth keeping as a fallback** for time.

#### A Warning About Rowan

- Lieve'tel cautioned against telling Rowan too much: she worries **his growing belief is at odds with his oath to the Arcana Pansophical**, whose purpose is to limit spellcasters — and beings — from growing too powerful.
- The tension: the Matron, now counted among the prime pantheon, is *exactly* the sort of ascendant power the Arcana would once have sought to suppress. (On necromancy, though, Rowan and the Matron would align — she abhors necromantic ritual as well.)

> Tomis: "You may have to tease that from him."

#### On the Age of Gods (timeline aside)

- The current year is in the **800s PD (post-Divergence)**, the reckoning that followed the Calamity. The Matron ascended a bit over **800 years ago**.
- Lieve'tel is a several-hundred-year-old elf (looks no older than ~400). **Rowan** came to this plane around the founding of Tal'Dorei, roughly **300–400 years ago** — younger than the Matron's ascension, not older. Tomis walked back his remark accordingly: his point was about the *stability* of the divine order, not that Rowan predates the Matron.

#### The Church, First Raised

- Before leaving, Tomis told Lieve'tel he wants a stronger presence for their church in **Tal'Dorei, perhaps Whitestone**. Lieve'tel: her support "is not required, nor would it be withheld"; the real obstacle is convincing the **Council of Whitestone**. She added that faith often takes root among the humblest people when belief rings true — it doesn't require a building permit; **it simply grows.**
- **Perception in her chambers:** the DM had Tomis look for "a certain something." He did not spot it. *(Unresolved hook.)*

> Tomis, on parting: "I can think of no one else I would trust as much as you to do it."

### Interrupting Breakfast — The Council of Whitestone

- Reunited with Tomis, the party once more **interrupted the Council's breakfast**.
- **Present:** Cassandra de Rolo, Timothy Locke, Kaily Shorthall, and Jubilee (Shorthalt). *(A few weeks since they last spoke.)*
- **Grimbold** mage-handed a bunch of grapes off the council's fruit platter: "I guess I'll start with these. As a reward."

#### The Question of Reward

- The reward is for defeating the **Nagpa** — a huge victory, and a domestic one: that Nagpa was charged with destroying Whitestone.
- Whitestone is cash-strapped (one city waging a continent's war, heavy casualties). It can spare **diamonds/residuum for perhaps one or two revivifications**, little raw gold. War and Aarakocra raids have choked trade.
- Alternatives offered: **raw materials (gems, art), credit, or land** the party can pawn elsewhere. *(The DM's "homework": decide what to ask for.)*

#### Tomis Petitions for a Church

- Tomis raised it directly — heartfelt, if premature: once this is over he wants to return to his ministry and help people through the Matron of Ravens; he'd originally thought of founding a church "back home in the Dawnmists." Would the council be open to one here?
- **Cassandra sat up straight. Insight: 11.** She answered carefully: she could raise it at the next meeting, but city infrastructure falls under the **Office of Ingenuity** (Percy de Rolo's purview) — no quick answer.
- Tomis clarified he sought **cooperation, if not a blessing**, on a sensitive subject, not a building permit. Cassandra: "How about we get through this mess, and then figure out what comes after." She noted the party has **already been deeded property north of the city** — what they develop there is their own discretion.

#### Wizard Towers and a "Council Members Only" Sign

- Grimbold floated a **wizard tower** attached to a temple to the Raven Queen. Cassandra, dryly: build it taller than **Allura's tower in Whitestone**, or it makes them look bad.
- **Grimbold history check: 28.** As a Cobalt Soul member he knows wizard-tower height is a perpetual ego contest, and that the Arcana Pansophical's highest seat — the **Realm Seer** — is the one position that "quite literally" needs the tallest tower.
- Tomis dryly suggested Grimbold just consult Allura and **match her height** rather than start an arms race. As the party left, Cassandra had a page make a **"Council members only"** sign for the door.

### Into the Bowels of the Castle — The Treasury

- A page led them (to **Kaily Shorthall**) down a staff-only administrative route, deep beneath Council Hill.
- **Arcana (Freya) / Observation (Grimbold, 27):** they passed through layers of **extremely powerful, non-subtle abjuration** — some protective, some meant to be *felt*, a subduing "weighted blanket." Comparable to the Cobalt Reserve's wards but more oppressive; a mix of **permanent wards and temporary ones raised only within roughly the past week**.
- **The descent strips all non-artifact magic:** Rowan's Mage Hand and Death Ward gone, his clothes/flowers dulled; Tomis's mount ceased. Grimbold wisely left **Grimcold** — his **simulacrum** — behind, since the anti-magic would unmake it.
- The staircase hides artificer etchings (recognized from Tomis's time with **Pyrrhus**) and likely traps; the layout is deliberately obscured.
- **Perception (antechamber):** once a natural cavern, now decorated — but something was **recently removed**: a stretch of dead wall where a painting should hang, less wear and soot. *(Unresolved.)*
- At the bottom: **Kaily Shorthall in work mode**, weighing a bag against reference charts. This is **Whitestone's Treasury** — guarded by about **eight hooded Pale Guard**.

#### Collecting the Reward

- **Kaily Shorthall — Curator of Fortune's Bounty** — took their requests (via Rowan): diamonds for resurrection (already in the works); credit toward magic items; the remainder in **art and gems** the party can carry and resell abroad; and a small ask — **plant one of each regional native plant on their deeded land** so a garden can grow, unmanicured, for Rowan. Kaily: "That's hardly worth a drop of ink in the ledger." Cost: **one silver.**
- Overheard (passive Perception 17+): Kaily directs a **bookish halfling clerk** to liquidate stored treasure — statuettes from the **Arcturusian delegation** and a long list of priceless art. *(Dragon-hoard-scale wealth — "When you have multiple dragon hoards in this city.")*
- **Total reward assessed at 135,000 gp.** Kaily, soberly: "Two — actually three — of you literally died for this." Rowan: "Having no liquid gold does not mean no liquid value."
- **Where to sell:** Vasselheim is a poor market (temples don't deal in coin, and are wary of powerful magic items), so the goods are bound for **Niirdal-Poc** (Grimbold's home), possibly via Emon. Kaily would hold back low-value bulk (silks) the ritzy market won't want, and pay them out in carriable form.

### The Reward, Settled

After a long party huddle, they landed on **35,000 gp of credit** for magic items + **100,000 gp in sellable goods**.

- **Magic items bought (on the 35k credit):**
  - **Eyes of the Bookworm** — for **Grimcold** (the simulacrum) to read quickly.
  - **Boots of Knock** — cheap; ends the party's long history of being stumped by locked doors.
  - **Null Chalk** — a 50-ft anti-magic line that repels magical creatures and blocks magical effects (breaks if the line is broken). Debated hard: it may be useless against the Adranachs (which carry their own anti-magic) and against a wizard who once hit them *inside* an anti-magic field. **Tomis** clinched it: if they never use it, they can just **sell it at Niirdal-Poc** — "it's still money."
  - **Blood Pact Pendant** — pairs two people who pre-sacrifice HP; when one **dies**, the other drops **unconscious** and the dead one revives with **2d10 + double the sacrificed HP** (a death ward that returns more than 1 HP, but drains the partner). The clever workaround: bind it not to a party member but to **Grimcold** (a construct — no death saves) or a willing church acolyte. Tomis: "This sounds fucked up beyond all reason," but he'd explore finding a willing devotee.
- **Passed on:** the **Wand of Whispers** (5th-level-or-lower only; 7 charges, one per spell level; strips only the verbal component). Not taken: **Ironwood Plate** (~19k), a **Spire Seed** (grows a 300-ft tower with a Guardian Dryad — "not tall enough for Grimbold," pure temptation).
- **Loading the bag:** the bag of holding is disabled by the Treasury wards, so **Freya hauled** the 135k of goods upstairs first — jeweled crowns, platinum filigree, gem-inlaid statuettes, **a black sapphire the size of a hand**, then bulk (large delicate glass panes, silks, spices). Everything previously in the bag was pulled out and distributed among the party.

She'd reorganized to hide that the party bought out most of her stock days ago; she'd only ever shown them a **list tailored to the Unbound** (which is why so much interested them).

### The Legend Lore — What the Rods Are

Jordana cast **Legend Lore** on the three incomplete rods the party took off the slain Nagpa (Tomis watched closely, hoping to learn the technique — he's cast it once, on a person, never an object). The vision:

> Inky void. Seven stars blink in at once. Below them, a single star strains upward toward the seven as some force tears it down — then all is wiped away. Three lines, echoing the three rods, pass into a monolithic being; one shatters and falls free, yet the being blinks from existence. Deeper still: three droplets of divinity poured into molten metal, cased in a Titan's forge. *But these aren't those* — these are **pieces of what could become those**, facsimiles of what came before, and the shattered remains of something three times the rods' length, housed in a place familiar to each of them: **the vault of Whitestone**, where they stood that very morning.

- **Reveal: these are trammel fragments.** One trammel used to bind **Vecna** shattered and broke out of him; the other two stayed within him and were **banished beyond the Divine Gate** with Vecna. The broken remains sat in Whitestone's vault — until recently **stolen by Iohezet ("iTunes") and his gang**.
- The three rods are **the same bottom-third of three *different* trammels**, purposely separated into thirds. There are three Nagpa — the **other two likely carry the other thirds**.
- They are **not broken but unfinished** — assembled they'd be "unholy," still lacking the crucial **drop of divinity**. The old trammels were forged when gods walked the earth; Vox Machina's later ones gained divinity only after they became **champions of each god** through trials.
- **Freya** rolled a nat 20 (28) Arcana on the read; **Grimbold** a nat 1 (11). ("Expertise, Arcana, already paying off.")
- The party swore Jordana to secrecy ("It might be safer for you if we didn't give you all the details") and left quickly.

### Commune with Deliverance

Tomis split to a small, run-down **shrine to the Matron of Ravens** in Whitestone (map point 12), down the street from the vibrantly-lit **Shrine of the Everlight** — whose faith, dovetailing with Pelor and the blessing of the Sun Tree, permeates Whitestone. The Matron's shrine had been run by charlatans extorting the faithful; now it sat cold and empty.

- Tomis forced the poorly-locked door (and **Mended** it shut behind him), swept away cobwebs with an old broom, and uncovered on the wall the faded, inked **porcelain face of the Matron**. He knelt and cast **Commune**, using a **twig collected from outside Deliverance's house in Jorenn Village** as the focus — enough to reach "fairly close."

> Tomis: "Deliverance — you tended the dead of Jorenn with gentle hands. I don't call you back into the cold of this world, but I ask you to carry three questions to her wings and bring back what she would have me know."

**The three questions and the Matron's answers, through Deliverance:**

1. *"Does the heart of the Grey Valley's power lie where the demon was slain?"* → **"There it is strongest, but it diffuses all."**
2. *"Would this be a place that you could be hidden? Would its demonic presence mask you from the Forgotten One?"* → **"Unlikely."**
3. *"Is Iohezet's death within our power to bring about?"* → **"Certainly."**

*(These are the questions drafted earlier: the Grey Valley's power is real but diffuse; it will not hide the Matron; and Iohezet can be killed.)*

### The Plan Debate, and an Augury

- Reunited, Tomis pitched returning to the Grey Valley — to harness its demonic energy for the Matron and to lure **Iohezet** to the valley where the Ravager city originated and kill him there. His theory: the demonic energy is part of what powers the Ravager castle-city (channeled by Iohezet), since — unlike Emon (League of Miracles arcane batteries) and the underwater city (ancient arcane sources) — the castle has no obvious power source. Maybe that's why the League wanted **Freya** as a battery. Freya: "I'd prefer not to be used as a battery."
- Rowan and Uriel were skeptical (asking the Matron to draw on demonic energy; whether it's even enough; why she hasn't done it before — Tomis: "Desperate times and desperate measures").
- **DM ruling:** the heart of the Grey Valley (the demon's death-site) is far from Jorenn Village — over the Umbra/Black Hills — and **unreachable on foot within the timeframe**.
- Tomis ritually cast **Augury** (his range now broad, ~24 hours, thanks to his collected foci), asking whether going to the Grey Valley to disrupt/stop the Ravager's city was a good plan. Result: **"Woe"** — the course *"would not achieve that result."*
- The party abandoned the Grey Valley plan: instead they'll **target the flying cities from Vasselheim and take out Iohezet there**.
